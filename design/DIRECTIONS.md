# Design directions: SlaughterFest 2026

Five design directions for the festival's website and online presence, built on the research in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md). Each one is drawn on the design canvas (desktop homepage, mobile homepage, A4 lineup poster and an Instagram 4:5 post): https://claude.ai/artifact/VwmHMwwCYKn3mfwEnicHib (private until shared).

**How they were made (19 Nov 2026).** Three generators each proposed five candidates from a different angle: the name (N), the metal scene (S) and the web medium (W). Three judges scored all 15 through separate lenses: scene credibility (would lifers wear it?), brand power (does it own the name?) and web and audience risk (tickets, accessibility, India, ad policy). A creative-director pass then cut them to five that differ in ground, palette logic, typeface, voice and reach, and grafted the best ideas from the rest into them.

**Everything factual is a placeholder.** Band names are invented; [DATES], [VENUE, CITY], [PRICE], [TICKET PARTNER], [PARTNER LOGO] and [ARTIST] are deliberately left open. Logos drawn on the canvas are type mock-ups: each direction assumes commissioned, credited human lettering for the final mark.

## The set

Five ways to wear a name that sounds like a crime report. Each has its own ground, palette logic, primary typeface and voice, so you are choosing a strategy, not a shade.

01 THE PLANT owns the word through the building: galvanised steel, a stencil sprayed on road cases, deadpan safety signage. It leans hardest on SLAUGHTER without ever showing a body, and it is the mainstream-crossover route a sponsor, venue manager or ad reviewer will sign off fastest.
02 HOUSEFULL owns the word as a film title: a Ramsay-era single-screen B-horror on poster yellow, with cast billing, a booking window and a HOUSEFULL board. It is the darkly humorous route and the only one that could only be Indian.
03 ILLEGIBLE is the premium, epic route: a commissioned thorn logo as a monument on void black, with its typed twin in cold Bodoni capitals. It is the one lifers will wear, and its DECIPHER switch means nobody is locked out.
04 COPY SHOP is the raw, underground route on photocopy paper: the loudest flyer the corner photostat shop ever ran, with mosh calls in the host city's script and a poster fans can copy for the price of a chai.
05 BATTLE VEST is the earnest, collectible route: indigo denim, a chrome back patch, a patch for every band and an Edition I patch with every ticket.

Between them they cover light grounds (copier paper, poster yellow), dark grounds (void black, indigo) and a mid ground (steel); humour from deadpan to camp to sincere pride; reach from the sponsor deck to the zine; and the whole scene, from black and death metal to grind, core, horror and trad. The primary typefaces never repeat (Big Shoulders Stencil, Alfa Slab One, Bodoni Moda, Archivo, Grenze), and neither do the colour logics: steel with a hazard signal, a printed yellow ground with red, cold monochrome with one ice blue, one-ink toner with a highlighter, and textile with metallic thread.

All five share the same plumbing, so whichever wins is ready to sell: a plain TICKETS button, all-in INR prices with base, GST and fee shown, sold-out tiers struck rather than deleted, typed lineups from one dataset with day filters, one control that pauses all motion plus a preferences panel, English with the host city's script, a 48px mark, numbered 4:5 templates with WhatsApp cuts, a BROADCAST cut in which the name is the only violent word, credited human lettering, and '2026' kept as live text so it can become 2027.

### Rules every direction shares
- Lineup, placeholders and homepage structure come only from the shared placeholder content; no real band, festival, film, studio, company, person or ticketing brand appears in the design.
- Every lockup reads SLAUGHTERFEST in full (never SLAUGHTER alone), and "2026" is always live text so it can become 2027.
- Menace in the art, welcome in the words: no blood, gore, bodies, carcasses, hooks, meat or weapons on the site and social; the name is the only violent word in the festival's own voice.
- Plain ticket language: the button says TICKETS; every themed tier carries a plain subtitle (Day pass / All days / Premium / Under-26); states read Coming soon / On sale / Almost gone / Sold out.
- Prices are all-in INR ("₹[PRICE] all-in", incl. GST and fees); sold-out tiers are struck, never deleted.
- No AI-slop tropes: no gradient-wash backgrounds, no left-border accent cards, no emoji.
- A countdown appears only once a real event date exists; until then a designed "dates soon" state.

## 01 THE PLANT

> The slaughterhouse as a building, never a body: a decommissioned plant reopened to process riffs at volume, its name stencilled on road cases and its rules posted with a deadpan health-and-safety straight face.

**Axes:** ground mid · register mixed · texture stark · reach crossover · built from N1, W1, N4, W2, W5, N5

**Why it could win.** It is the safest way to keep the word's teeth. The threat lives in steel and stencil, not flesh, so it clears Meta and Google, vegetarian India, venue managers and sponsors (workwear, audio, energy) while still looking heavier than anything in the feed. Its vocabulary doubles as real operations language (gate passes, halls, andon status, shift notices, site induction), so wayfinding, welfare and honest pricing come built in. The stencil can be cut and sprayed for real on road cases, so the brand lives on the backline, the most scene-native surface a festival owns. The brand and web judges both put this territory in their five.

**What it risks.** Grey and yellow can read as a techno warehouse, a workwear catalogue or a safety poster if the stencil gets small and polite; lifers will enjoy the deadpan more than they wear it, and hazard yellow is not unclaimed (Aftershock and Brutal Assault used it in 2026), so the stencil, the road cases and Night Shift have to carry ownership.

### What the judges warned about
- **Scene (5/10):** Clever and sponsor-proof, but a hazard-yellow stencil on galvanised grey reads first as a techno warehouse, Off-White streetwear or a site-safety poster, so lifers will enjoy the deadpan joke ('the circle pit runs one way') more than they will wear it; its credibility comes from the road cases, the cold-store menace and the black Night Shift mode, not the andon board.
- **Brand (7/10):** The stencilled SLAUGHTER on galvanised steel reads like the sign on an abattoir door without showing a single body, and gate passes, halls, ID badges and andon status make it the most complete wayfinding-to-merch system in the field, but hazard yellow already appears on Aftershock's 2026 poster and Brutal Assault's 2026 social cards, and a small, polite stencil drifts toward a techno night or a workwear catalogue.
- **Web (6/10):** The web plumbing is excellent: six plain-language pass states, an andon strip that always prints the phase in words, a Night Shift dark mode, Hindi signage, and a stencil that fits a phone at 64px (281px wide). But the hero keeps the abattoir's own furniture (an overhead trolley rail with hanging name tags, a strip-curtain cold-store door, a COLD STORE hall), which a hostile reader in vegetarian India can screenshot as slaughterhouse imagery.

## 02 HOUSEFULL

> SLAUGHTERFEST as a Ramsay-era single-screen B-horror on opening night: a hand-painted Sivakasi poster, cast billing for the lineup, a booking window for tickets and a HOUSEFULL board when they go.

**Axes:** ground light · register humorous · texture ornate · reach mainstream · built from W3, N2, N3, N4, W2, W5

**Why it could win.** It is the only direction that could only be Indian, yet cinema reads instantly abroad. As a film title the name becomes a promise rather than a problem, film-credit billing ends size fights and gives home-grown openers real credit, and the ticket path speaks fluent Indian box office: FIRST DAY FIRST SHOW, FAST FILLING, HOUSEFULL, 'No black tickets'. Yellow, red and maroon shout from any dark feed, and a commissioned sign painter gives it credited human craft and endless process content. It is the set's darkly funny route, and the brand and web judges both picked it.

**What it risks.** It sits closest to the filmi mainstream that Indian metal defines itself against (the scene judge scored it 4): without hall-black discipline, a heavy straight lineup and a real sign painter it slides into a Bollywood party or a desi-kitsch cafe, the yellow ground glares at night, and international fans may miss the references.

It makes the festival collectible from the first sale and turns the reveal campaign into one growing object: THE VEST SO FAR, reposted after every wave, and SEW YOUR VEST, a planner that exports a WhatsApp profile picture. Earnest pride is a register none of the other four has, and denim and apparel partners have an obvious home.

**What it risks.** Denim and chrome can read as trad or 'dad metal' to extreme fans, a free patch is already Wacken's signature, and stitched SVG on twill is the heaviest texture load of the five on low-end phones.

### What the judges warned about
- **Scene (8/10):** The battle vest is metal's most universal subcultural object, and a woven Edition I patch with every ticket, a patch-wall lineup, straight lockups instead of biker rockers and moderation of hate-symbol patches all show real insider knowledge; the risks are theme-park denim skeuomorphism, a chrome logo set in a font where lifers expect hand lettering, and tells like 'WASH COLD' (nobody washes a vest).
- **Brand (5/10):** The vest is metal's most universal badge and a yearly Edition patch builds a real collecting ritual, but denim and chrome read as trad or dad metal, free patches are already Wacken's signature, and nothing in it belongs to the name SLAUGHTERFEST.
- **Web (6/10):** It has a clear gold TICKETS cell (10.6:1), every metal fan recognises a battle vest, and ad risk is low. But full-bleed denim twill with turbulence filters plus stitched SVG patches is the heaviest texture load for low-end Android phones. 'GET YOUR PATCH' muddles what you are buying. It names no Devanagari font, and the 360px mobile back patch is wider than a 343px column.

### The build brief
#### 1. Concept & world
The battle vest, built patch by patch, is metal's most universal object. SLAUGHTERFEST becomes the back patch every lifer wants to earn: an indigo denim site, the name as a chrome-lettered oval back patch, every band a woven patch, a never-reprinted Edition I patch with every ticket. Earnest trad-metal glory: pride, not war, every tribe on the wall.
World: tickets = PATCHES (phases are STITCHES); lineup = THE PATCH WALL; stages = BACK PATCH, FRONT PANEL, THE POCKET; VIP = STUDDED; planner = MY VEST; newsletter = THE PATCH REPORT; community = THE VESTED; welfare = THE TAILORS ('we fix things'); edition = EDITION I. Merch: logo tee, lineup back print; UNCUT art merch-only.

#### 2. Wordmark
'SLAUGHTERFEST' in Grenze 900 caps, tracking 0, straight (arched rockers read as biker colours). SVG text with a vertical chrome linearGradient (#F4F7FA 0, #9DB3CB 0.46, #24324D 0.50 hard horizon, #5E7088 0.56, #E8EEF3 1), a 2.5px #F2B632 stroke (paint-order stroke) and a #0B0B0D copy offset (6, 8) behind. Width = 7.53 x size.
It sits in an oval back patch: patch-black ellipse, merrowed edge (10px gold stroke, dasharray 1.6 1.4), running stitch (2px, dasharray 7 5), gold 'EDITION I' banner in Anton, and '2026' as live Grenze 800 bone text above the name (next: 2027, Edition II patch). Desktop patch 1000x520, name 122px; mobile patch 343px wide, name 40px; poster patch 580px wide, name 68px.
Header: name 26px, chrome via background-clip: text. Avatar 48px: round woven patch, chrome 'SF', gold merrowed edge. Commission the lettering, embroider it, film the machine.

#### 3. Type system
Grenze 900 caps: wordmark, headliners (64px desktop; 36px mobile, where 17 characters are 336px), tier 2 (44/28px), section heads (56/34px). Anton caps: strip patches, nav 16px +0.06em, buttons 18px, tiers 3-4 (30/20 desktop, 20/15 mobile), prices. Chivo 400/700: body 17px/1.6 and labels. Eczar 800: host-city patch line 'स्लॉटरफ़ेस्ट'. All carry ₹; arrows in SVG.

#### 4. Colour usage
Indigo #1D2A44 ground with twill; patch black #0E0E10 for patches and cards; bone #EEE7D6 body (11.6:1 on indigo); chrome steel #9DB3CB secondary (6.6:1). Thread gold #F2B632 is the accent: TICKETS fill with black text (10.6:1), stitches, numerals, banners. Red thread #E0353F only for the sold-out cross-stitch, never text. Chrome lives on the wordmark only.

#### 5. Textures & motifs
- Twill: #1D2A44 with repeating-linear-gradient(45deg, rgba(255,255,255,0.05) 0 2px, transparent 2px 6px) and repeating-linear-gradient(-45deg, rgba(0,0,0,0.18) 0 1px, transparent 1px 5px); one feTurbulence fade at 10%, hero only.
- Patches: one SVG symbol per shape (shield, oval, strip), merrowed edge and running stitch, reused; no per-patch filters.
- Pyramid studs: 14px squares at 45 degrees, linear-gradient(135deg, #E8EEF3, #5E7088): bullets and a header row.
- Enamel pins for annotations: rounded chips, 2px gold rim, white gloss.
- Woven care label in the footer: bone on black, zigzag ends.

#### 6. Motion

The QUIET pin stops all motion. Reduced motion or Save-Data: the vest renders already filled, which is also the share image.

## What didn't make the cut

THE S IS SILENT (N5, 6.0): the cleverest play on the name and the friendliest face for sponsors, but a wordmark that reads LAUGHTER tells lifers the festival is a joke about its own name (scene 3), the pun does not survive in Devanagari, and with HOUSEFULL and COPY SHOP the set already has its humour. Kept: SILENT MODE lives on as every route's preferences panel, and SIGN OF THE HORNS (Indian Sign Language taught by paid, credited Deaf creators) should run whichever route wins.

MOSH CALL (S5, 6.0): the best web plumbing in the field, but white and electric blue read as a sneaker drop and the system would work under any festival's name. Its bilingual calls, fitted Archivo widths and pit ethics now power COPY SHOP.

FINAL CUT (N2, 6.0) and REWIND (W2, 5.3): horror cinema is real death-metal heritage, but two cinema routes would waste a slot and VHS is the decade's most crowded retro look. HOUSEFULL takes the billing block, the implied menace, the sequel numbering (REEL I, REEL II) and RUNTIME; REWIND's numbered spine strip is worth testing on the winner's Instagram grid.

HEAVY INDUSTRY (W1, 5.7): merged into THE PLANT, which takes its machinery-only imagery, E-STOP, SF / I plate and SHIFT NOTICE numbering.

THE LEDGER (W5, 5.3): the most honest ticket path, but its quiet, bookish register undersells a mosh crowd. 'Struck, never deleted' tiers and visible corrections are now in all five.

LOW SUN (S3, 5.3): beautiful, but doom at golden hour undersells a death, thrash and core bill and touches nothing in the name. The amp-monolith shirt is a good merch idea for any route.

SLAUGHTERFEST & CO. (N3, 4.7): the best inversion line ('we butcher riffs, not animals'), but a butcher's fascia is a communal flashpoint in India. Its itemised all-in receipt lives on in every ticket card, and its NOW SERVING queue in HOUSEFULL's booking window.

THE TAPESTRY (N4, 4.7): the cleverest reading of the word, but a figurative frieze cannot be hand-built well here, and 'great slaughter on both sides' echoes communal violence. Its pit line survives, edited, as ILLEGIBLE's manifesto, and its RAMPART is now an accessible raised-platform line in every ticket ladder.

SERIES I (W4, 4.3): lifers read rarity tiers as a billing insult and blind packs as loot boxes. Its numbered reveal counter and Save-Data static fallback are now standard.
